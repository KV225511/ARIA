## Synthesis

The literature occupies each component of ARIA’s intended design but leaves the repository’s exact systems question only partially occupied. Automated interview models already process multimodal answers and predict ratings; adaptive testing already maintains latent-state uncertainty and learns item-selection policies; offline RL already offers conservative learning and evaluation machinery. The narrower opening is therefore not “AI interviews” or “adaptive questioning” in isolation. It is the reproducible integration and audit of explicit skill-state beliefs, identity-isolated synthetic trajectories, and a conservative offline question policy, with claim boundaries visible in the artifacts.

The closest new counterexample is PolyInterview, which combines role-conditioned planning, answer-aware follow-ups, and multimodal feedback. It materially weakens any first-system claim. ARIA can still contribute a different artifact: an explicit state/action contract, calibration lineage, locked synthetic evaluation, and diagnostic OPE. That contribution remains prototype-level until real respondents, construct-valid labels, subgroup/accessibility analyses, and fresh learned-policy rollouts are available.
